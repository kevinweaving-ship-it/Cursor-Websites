## Why people chose the Polish recreational flag in 2020–2021

These are **documented reasons the Polish flag was being sold and used in that period**. They are the context for Bruce’s boat. They are **not** proof of his motive.

### 1. New EU recreational register, open to non-residents

Poland’s 12 April 2018 Act, implemented by the 17 April 2020 regulation and switched on **1 August 2020**, created one national register for seagoing and inland craft ≤24 m. A 20 m wooden motor vessel fits.

A 2026 industry comparison of current rules states that REJA24 accepts **any nationality, with no Polish residence requirement**, using an address for service and a Polish home port. France’s pleasure register, by contrast, expects an address in France (or *élection de domicile*) and at least 50% EU/EEA ownership.

That is the structural reason a French-resident owner could put a Mediterranean boat on the Polish recreational flag without moving to Poland.

Sources: [gov.pl REJA24](https://www.gov.pl/web/infrastruktura/system-reja24---rejestr-jachtow-i-innych-jednostek-plywajacych-o-dlugosci-do-24-m2) · [Dz.U. 2020 poz. 763](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2020/763/text.html) · [Gestión de Banderas, 27 Oct 2026](https://gestiondebanderas.com/en/polish-or-french-flag/)

### 2. Belgian and Dutch “easy flags” closed at the same moment

A 22 May 2021 French-language survey of the new Polish offer is explicit about the market it was replacing:

- Since 1993 EU citizens may use another member state’s pleasure register.
- **Belgium** had been popular (low forfait tax, light safety kit). Belgian authorities then required a **Belgian-resident** owner or co-owner, which pushed non-residents off the Belgian flag.
- The common **Dutch ICP** is a cadastral-style pleasure certificate, not a nationality document. French customs **stopped treating it as a document of nationality from 2019**; Portugal was warning Dutch-ICP boats as well. A hull left on ICP in France was at risk of being treated as stateless.
- Poland then advertised: internationally valid nationality document, low one-off fee (then ~€499, or ~€648 with MMSI), **no annual tax**, **no periodic safety inspection** beyond initial CE evidence, **lifetime / no renewal**, short issue times.

MarinePoland later described the result: by 2026 REJA24 held well over 100,000 vessels, **a significant share foreign-owned**. A 2026 draft reform (Sejm print 2995, not yet law as of 27 Oct 2026) would end lifetime registration and add five-year confirmation — which itself confirms that “register once, forget it” was the original selling point.

Sources: [Ecopeco, 22 May 2021](https://ecopeco.org/2021/05/22/la-question-juridique-pour-bateau-le-pavillon-polonais/) · [MarinePoland on REJA24 reform, 31 Oct 2026](https://www.marinepoland.com/the-polish-recreational-flag-following-the-amendment-proposed-changes-to-reja24-2605)

### 3. Brexit, if the previous flag or company was British

Sunrise Adventures Ltd was a **UK** company, dissolved April 2019. The hull came from Scotland. After 31 Dec 2020 a UK-flagged pleasure boat based in the EU no longer carried an EU flag.

Bruce’s Companies House nationality is **French**, residence France. He did not need a UK flag to own the boat. Brexit is a **plausible** reason to leave a British flag (if she still had one) for an EU recreational flag in 2020–21. It is **not** proven that she was still British when REJA24 opened.

### 4. What Polish flag does *not* automatically explain

- **French annual boat tax (TAEMUP).** A French resident with a foreign-flagged pleasure boat still pays TAEMUP. Switching France → Poland does not, on current comparison tables, remove that tax for a French resident.
- **Spanish matriculation tax.** Irrelevant unless the boat was used as a Spanish-resident craft.
- **Commercial charter licensing.** French passenger-charter rules are a separate regime from the flag. A recreational Polish flag does not by itself authorise commercial charters in France or Greece. If the Antibes charter operation had already wound down before the move to Vlycho, a cheap recreational flag is a better fit than a commercial one.

## What would apply to *this* boat

**Supported, not proven:**

1. She is ~20 m, so she **could** use REJA24.
2. The Polish flag is **on her at Vlycho by 18 Dec 2023**, and local reporting implies it was already her flag while she sat there for years.
3. The owner Greece later named is a **French association**. An association can be a REJA24 owner (legal person). Putting title in ECO-VIE and the flag in Poland is a coherent structure. It is still **not** shown on a Polish extract.
4. Timing lines up with **REJA24 going live (Aug 2020)** plus **Belgian/Dutch options dying (2019–2021)** plus **Brexit (2020)**. That is the period a French-based owner of an ex-Scottish boat would be shopping for a new EU recreational flag.

**Not shown:**

- The date Poland first issued `POL000AYP` (the number itself is now in the Greek PDF).
- Whether Bruce, Sunrise Adventures Ltd, ECO-VIE, or someone else was the first Polish owner.
- Whether money changed hands.
- Any MMSI tied to this hull (UKE ship-station lists have several unrelated `SUNRISE` radio licences).

Until the `odpis pełny` is in hand, the honest answer to “when” is **between 1 Aug 2020 and ~Dec 2021**, and the honest answer to “why” is **the Polish recreational flag was the open, cheap, lifetime EU alternative after Belgium, the Dutch ICP, and the UK flag became poor options — and that is the hypothesis, not the motive on file**.
